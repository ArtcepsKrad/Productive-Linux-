
# Productive Linux

## The Problem: Video-Based Dopamine

One of the biggest problems I see in the current generation is the rise of **video-based entertainment and instant stimulation**.

I think about digital stimulation in three broad stages:

### 1. Text-Based Dopamine

**Examples:**

* Newspapers
* Magazines
* Books
* Educational materials

Text requires active reading and imagination. It is generally slower and gives the user more control over the pace of consumption.

### 2. Audio-Based Dopamine

**Examples:**

* iPods
* Tape recorders
* Music
* Parties
* Trips

Audio introduced a different advantage: **you can consume it while doing something else.**

You can listen to music, podcasts, or educational material while walking, exercising, traveling, cleaning, or doing other activities.

### 3. Video-Based Dopamine

**Examples:**

* Video games
* Movies
* YouTube
* Short-form videos
* Reels

This is where I see a major problem.

Video demands visual attention. Unlike audio, it is difficult to consume video while simultaneously doing meaningful work.

The problem becomes especially severe with **short-form content**, where platforms continuously provide another piece of content with almost no friction.

---

# The Facebook Example

This transition can even be seen in social media.

Facebook originally focused heavily on **text-based posts**.

Then came:

**Text → Images → Video → Reels**

The amount of visual stimulation increased over time.

The goal of Productive Linux is not to completely eliminate technology or entertainment.

Instead, the goal is to create an environment where **high-value activities are easier to access and low-value distractions are harder to access.**

---

# My Solution

My current approach is:

> **Avoid video-based dopamine as much as reasonably possible.**

The problem is uncontrolled consumption.

Therefore, the goal is **controlled access rather than absolute prohibition.**

---

# My Journey

## 1. Windows

I originally used Windows as my main operating system.

I used **Cold Turkey Blocker** to block YouTube for specific periods of time.

I intentionally didn't block YouTube permanently.

### Why?

Because YouTube can also be an extremely useful learning platform.

A permanent ban would remove both:

**Low-value entertainment**

and

**High-value education**

I wanted to keep the second while reducing the first.

---

# 2. The Video Game Problem

Then I faced another problem:

> **Video games were still easily available.**

Even if I controlled YouTube, I could still access a huge amount of video-game entertainment.

So I started looking for a different environment.

---

# 3. Moving to Linux

I moved to **Arch Linux**.

Linux changed the environment significantly.

One of the motivations was to create a system where the software I actually needed could remain available while unnecessary entertainment became less convenient.

However, a new problem appeared.

---

# 4. The Blocking Problem

On Windows, I had Cold Turkey.

On Linux, I didn't have an equivalent solution that provided everything I wanted.

I needed a blocker that could provide:

* Strong uninstall protection
* Browser-level protection
* Automatic scheduling
* Resistance against simply disabling the blocker

That's when I came across **Digital Zen**.

---

# 5. Digital Zen

Digital Zen had several features that caught my attention.

### 1. Uninstall Protection

The blocker could protect itself from being immediately removed.

### 2. Browser Extension Protection

The browser extension couldn't simply be removed whenever I wanted to bypass the restriction.

### 3. Automatic Scheduling

Restrictions could be automatically activated according to a schedule.

These features addressed the problem I had encountered after moving to Linux.

But there was still another problem:

> **Movie**

And that leads to the next stage of my journey — and eventually to the idea behind **Productive Linux**.
